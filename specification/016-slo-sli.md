# SLOs and SLIs

**Purpose.** This document defines the framework within which Heimdall's Service Level Indicators (SLIs) and Service Level Objectives (SLOs) are expressed, measured, reviewed, and enforced. It fixes the per-cell matrix on which SLIs are observed, the canonical SLI definitions (latency percentiles, error rate, availability, cache hit rate, DNSSEC validation rate), the canonical SLO commitments derived from the regression-threshold framework already fixed by [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md), the error-budget policy that governs how budget burn is computed and how exhaustion is recovered from, and the reporting cadence that keeps the framework reviewed in line with the project governance. It does not redefine the per-cell `(role, transport)` matrix, the calibration procedure, the regression thresholds, the readiness predicates, or the structured-event and metrics catalogue; those questions are settled in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md), [`012-runtime-operations.md`](012-runtime-operations.md), and [`007-threat-model.md`](007-threat-model.md) respectively.

**Status.** Draft. The framework is fixed; the concrete absolute targets in section 5 are derived by construction from the Sprint 35 calibration artefact and from the regression-threshold framework in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md). Items that require additional decisions beyond what is already settled in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md) are listed in section 10.

**Requirement categories.** `SLI`, `SLO`.

For the project-wide principles that frame these requirements (security non-negotiable, performance as the primary guide, "Assume Nothing"), see [`../CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md). For specification-wide conventions, see [`README.md`](README.md). For the role model on which the matrix is defined, see [`001-server-roles.md`](001-server-roles.md). For the transport profiles enumerated by the matrix, see [`002-transports.md`](002-transports.md). For the per-cell matrix, the calibration procedure, the regression thresholds, and the pinned reference-implementation cycle from which SLO commitments derive, see [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md), in particular `PERF-001` through `PERF-005`, `PERF-033`, `PERF-034`, and `PERF-037`. For the readiness predicates that define when a Heimdall instance is observable as available, see [`012-runtime-operations.md`](012-runtime-operations.md), in particular `OPS-024` and `OPS-042`. For the observability surface that emits the underlying signals on which SLIs are computed, see [`007-threat-model.md`](007-threat-model.md), in particular `THREAT-080` through `THREAT-083`. For the DNSSEC outcome vocabulary on which the DNSSEC-validation SLI is computed, see [`005-dnssec-policy.md`](005-dnssec-policy.md). For the segregated-cache model on which the cache-hit-rate SLI is computed, see [`004-cache-policy.md`](004-cache-policy.md).

## 1. Scope

This document applies to every running Heimdall instance regardless of which combination of roles is active, and to every cell of the `(role, transport)` matrix fixed by `PERF-001` through `PERF-003` in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md). SLIs are observed per cell. SLO commitments are expressed per cell. The error-budget policy is computed per cell. Aggregations across cells are operator-facing reporting conveniences and MUST NOT replace per-cell commitments.

The scope of this document is operational measurement and commitment. Implementation of the underlying metric collection, the storage of historical SLI samples, and the alerting pipeline are out of scope here and are covered by the observability requirements of [`007-threat-model.md`](007-threat-model.md). Reproducibility of the bench, the calibration of the initial absolute targets, and the comparative ambition against reference implementations are out of scope here and are covered by [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md).

## 2. Relationship to the performance-targets framework

The SLO framework defined in this document is the operational projection of the performance-targets framework fixed by [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md). The performance-targets framework defines targets and regression thresholds against a Sprint 35 calibration baseline that drives CI gates on every change to the source tree; this document defines the deployment-side commitments against the same dimensions, derived from the same baseline, but expressed in language and at a cadence suited to operations rather than to CI.

Concretely: every SLO commitment in section 5 MUST be derivable from one or more `PERF-*` requirements in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md). A new SLO commitment that has no corresponding `PERF-*` derivation MUST NOT be introduced without a coordinated change to both documents. The reverse does not hold: not every `PERF-*` requirement gives rise to an SLO commitment, because some performance dimensions (memory footprint, CPU efficiency, concurrent connection count) are CI-gated regressions rather than user-facing service-level commitments.

## 3. Definitions

For the purposes of this document, the following definitions apply.

- **Cell.** The pair `(role, transport)` as defined by `PERF-001` and `PERF-002` in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md). The matrix spans the three roles defined by [`001-server-roles.md`](001-server-roles.md) and the six transport profiles defined by `PERF-002`.
- **SLI sample.** A single measurement of an SLI over its defined evaluation window.
- **SLI window.** The time interval over which an SLI sample is computed. Distinct SLIs have distinct windows, fixed in section 4.
- **SLO target.** The threshold value that an SLI is committed to meet, expressed as either a numerical bound (latency) or a fraction (error rate, availability, cache hit rate, DNSSEC validation rate).
- **Compliance window.** The rolling time interval over which compliance with an SLO is evaluated. The default compliance window is **30 days** unless otherwise specified per SLO.
- **Error budget.** The complement of the SLO target over the compliance window. For an availability SLO of `99.9%` over 30 days, the error budget is `0.1%` of 30 days, i.e. approximately 43.2 minutes of allowed unavailability per 30-day window.
- **Burn rate.** The rate at which the error budget is being consumed, expressed as a multiple of the steady-state rate that would exhaust the budget exactly at the end of the compliance window. A burn rate of `1` exhausts the budget at the window boundary; a burn rate of `10` exhausts the budget in `1/10` of the window.

## 4. SLI definitions

### 4.1 Normative requirements

- **SLI-001.** Heimdall MUST emit, per cell of the matrix fixed by `PERF-001` through `PERF-003` in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md), an end-to-end query-latency SLI at the p50 percentile, computed over a sliding 5-minute window from the metric histograms exposed by `THREAT-081` and `THREAT-083` in [`007-threat-model.md`](007-threat-model.md). The measurement boundary MUST be identical to the one fixed by `PERF-007`: from the point at which the query is observable on the listening socket to the point at which the response is handed back to the operating system for transmission.

- **SLI-002.** Heimdall MUST emit, per cell, an end-to-end query-latency SLI at the p99 percentile, computed over a sliding 5-minute window under the same measurement boundary as `SLI-001`. The p99 SLI is the primary latency SLI for user-facing commitments under section 5.

- **SLI-003.** Heimdall MUST emit, per cell, an end-to-end query-latency SLI at the p99.9 percentile, computed over a sliding 5-minute window under the same measurement boundary as `SLI-001`. The p99.9 SLI is the tail-latency SLI used for early detection of outlier degradation.

- **SLI-004.** Heimdall MUST emit, per cell, a query error-rate SLI computed as the fraction of queries within a sliding 5-minute window whose response is one of `SERVFAIL`, a transport-level error (e.g. connection reset, stream reset, QUIC connection close with error), or a structurally invalid response. The denominator MUST be the count of all queries admitted past ACL and rate-limit evaluation in the same window. Queries denied by ACL under `THREAT-033` through `THREAT-047` in [`007-threat-model.md`](007-threat-model.md), and queries rejected by rate limiting under `THREAT-048` through `THREAT-060` in [`007-threat-model.md`](007-threat-model.md), MUST NOT count toward the error-rate numerator or denominator: they are policy responses, not service failures.

- **SLI-005.** Heimdall MUST emit, per cell, an availability SLI computed over 5-minute windows. A 5-minute window MUST be classified as available if and only if at least `99%` of the queries admitted in that window were answered within the cell's p99 latency target as fixed by `SLO-001`. The availability SLI MUST be the fraction of 5-minute windows over the compliance window that satisfy the per-window availability predicate; the compliance window defaults to 30 days per section 3. Windows in which fewer than 100 queries were admitted MUST be excluded from the availability calculation; low-traffic windows MUST NOT count as either available or unavailable.

- **SLI-006.** Heimdall MUST emit, on the recursive resolver role, a cache hit-rate SLI computed as the fraction of queries on that role's cell within a sliding 5-minute window that were served from the recursive cache without contacting an upstream authoritative server. The numerator MUST count cache hits as defined by the segregated-cache model in [`004-cache-policy.md`](004-cache-policy.md); the denominator MUST count all queries admitted on the recursive resolver role in the same window. Aggressive NSEC/NSEC3 synthesis hits under `DNSSEC-011` (synthesis from cached negative proofs) MUST count toward the cache hit-rate numerator: they are answered without an upstream query.

- **SLI-007.** Heimdall MUST emit, on the forwarder role, a cache hit-rate SLI computed under the same definition as `SLI-006`, with the numerator counting hits on the forwarder cache and the denominator counting all queries admitted on the forwarder role. The forwarder cache hit-rate is computed independently of the recursive cache hit-rate per `CACHE-001` and `CACHE-002` in [`004-cache-policy.md`](004-cache-policy.md); the two SLIs MUST NOT be aggregated across roles.

- **SLI-008.** Heimdall MUST emit, on every cell whose role validates DNSSEC (the recursive resolver role and the forwarder role per `DNSSEC-*` in [`005-dnssec-policy.md`](005-dnssec-policy.md)), a DNSSEC validation rate SLI computed over a sliding 5-minute window. The SLI MUST be reported as the distribution of validation outcomes across the four states defined by `DNSSEC-010` in [`005-dnssec-policy.md`](005-dnssec-policy.md): `secure`, `insecure`, `bogus`, and `indeterminate`. Each outcome MUST be exposed as a separate fraction of the total responses subject to DNSSEC validation in the window.

- **SLI-009.** The error-rate SLI under `SLI-004` MUST distinguish `SERVFAIL` responses that result from a `bogus` DNSSEC validation outcome (per `DNSSEC-013` in [`005-dnssec-policy.md`](005-dnssec-policy.md)) from `SERVFAIL` responses caused by upstream unreachability or other transport-level conditions. The `bogus`-driven `SERVFAIL` count MUST be reported as a separate sub-counter; it MUST still be included in the aggregate error-rate numerator under `SLI-004`, because from the client perspective both outcomes are observed as `SERVFAIL`, but the sub-counter MUST be available for diagnostic distinction.

- **SLI-010.** Heimdall MUST emit, per cell, a "readiness" SLI computed over a sliding 5-minute window as the fraction of time in the window during which the `GET /readyz` predicate fixed by `OPS-024` and `OPS-042` in [`012-runtime-operations.md`](012-runtime-operations.md) evaluated to ready. The readiness SLI is observed from outside the process by the observability endpoint; it is the platform-level complement to the in-process query-latency and error-rate SLIs and is the SLI that orchestrators (Kubernetes, systemd, equivalent) consume directly for service health.

- **SLI-011.** Every SLI required by `SLI-001` through `SLI-010` MUST be derivable from metrics exposed by `THREAT-081` and `THREAT-083` in [`007-threat-model.md`](007-threat-model.md). No SLI MUST require a new metric beyond those already required by the observability surface; the SLI framework is a consumption layer over the metrics layer, not a separate emission surface. When an SLI requires an aggregation or a derivation not directly computable from the exposed metrics, the consuming pipeline (Prometheus, the OpenTelemetry collector, or an equivalent) MUST perform the derivation; Heimdall itself MUST NOT pre-compute and expose SLI values.

- **SLI-012.** The hot-path discipline of `THREAT-085` and `THREAT-086` in [`007-threat-model.md`](007-threat-model.md) MUST apply uniformly to every metric emission that feeds an SLI under `SLI-001` through `SLI-011`. SLI emission MUST NOT introduce a new contention point on the worker threads or a new emission cost on the normal-path query flow. SLI windows are computed by the consuming pipeline from the metrics already exposed by `THREAT-083`; the Heimdall process itself MUST NOT maintain rolling-window state for SLI purposes.

## 5. SLO commitments

### 5.1 Normative requirements

- **SLO-001.** For every cell of the matrix, the p99 query-latency SLI (`SLI-002`) over the compliance window MUST NOT exceed the cell's calibrated p99 baseline by more than the regression threshold fixed by `PERF-037` in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md). The calibrated p99 baseline per cell is the value recorded by the Sprint 35 calibration run under `PERF-033` and `PERF-034` and stored in `benches/baselines/`. The deployment-side SLO commitment is therefore: `p99(deployment) ≤ 1.10 × p99(baseline)`. Absolute numerical p99 ms values per cell are not fixed by this document; they are derived per cell from the calibration artefact under `PERF-034` and are tracked at the project's operational dashboard rather than in the specification.

- **SLO-002.** For every cell, the p99.9 query-latency SLI (`SLI-003`) over the compliance window MUST NOT exceed the cell's calibrated p99.9 baseline by more than the regression threshold fixed by `PERF-037`. The commitment is: `p99.9(deployment) ≤ 1.15 × p99.9(baseline)`. The 1.15 multiplier reflects the 15% threshold in `PERF-037` for p99.9 and recognises that tail-latency variance is structurally higher than the p99 envelope.

- **SLO-003.** For every cell, the query error-rate SLI (`SLI-004`) over the compliance window MUST be less than or equal to **0.1%** (one error response per one thousand admitted queries). The 0.1% commitment is the steady-state target; it does not include `SERVFAIL` responses caused by `bogus` DNSSEC outcomes, which are policy-correct responses to adversarial or misconfigured upstream data and are tracked separately via `SLI-009`.

- **SLO-004.** For every cell, the availability SLI (`SLI-005`) over the compliance window MUST be greater than or equal to **99.99%** of qualifying 5-minute windows (the "four nines" commitment), computed over a 30-day rolling compliance window. At 99.99% over 30 days, the error budget is approximately **4.32 minutes** of unavailable windows per 30-day period.

- **SLO-005.** For the cell `(recursive resolver, *)`, the recursive cache hit-rate SLI (`SLI-006`) over the compliance window MUST be greater than or equal to **80%** as the long-run steady-state commitment for a fully-warm cache under representative workload. The 80% commitment is the operational floor; deployments serving substantially atypical workloads (e.g. dominated by NXDOMAIN floods or random-subdomain attacks) are expected to fall below this floor during the attack and to recover to the floor afterwards, and such transient excursions are not SLO violations as long as the rolling 30-day mean exceeds the floor.

- **SLO-006.** For the cell `(forwarder, *)`, the forwarder cache hit-rate SLI (`SLI-007`) over the compliance window MUST be greater than or equal to **70%** as the long-run steady-state commitment. The lower floor (70% versus 80% on the recursive resolver) reflects that forwarder workloads more often serve a narrower upstream-driven population whose cacheability is dominated by the upstream's response TTLs rather than by the breadth of the recursive walk.

- **SLO-007.** For every cell whose role validates DNSSEC, the DNSSEC validation rate SLI (`SLI-008`) over the compliance window MUST satisfy: the `bogus` outcome fraction MUST be less than or equal to **0.5%** of total validations in the window; the `secure` plus `insecure` plus `indeterminate` outcome fractions MUST sum to at least **99.5%**. The 0.5% `bogus` ceiling protects against a regression in the validator implementation that would cause spurious `bogus` outcomes against well-signed zones; it does not constrain the rate at which genuinely bogus zones are detected, because the latter is a property of the public DNS rather than of Heimdall.

- **SLO-008.** For every cell, the readiness SLI (`SLI-010`) over the compliance window MUST be greater than or equal to **99.95%** of time-in-window during which the `GET /readyz` predicate evaluated to ready, computed over a 30-day rolling compliance window. At 99.95% over 30 days, the readiness error budget is approximately **21.6 minutes** per 30-day period. The readiness SLO is intentionally more relaxed than the availability SLO under `SLO-004` because readiness includes external dependencies (Redis reachability, upstream reachability for the forwarder role, DNSSEC trust-anchor initialisation for validating roles) whose failure is not always within Heimdall's control.

- **SLO-009.** The SLO commitments fixed by `SLO-001` through `SLO-008` MUST be enforced uniformly per cell. A cell that meets every SLO except one MUST be classified as out of compliance on the single failing SLO; SLO compliance MUST NOT be aggregated across SLOs of the same cell, and MUST NOT be aggregated across cells of the same SLO.

- **SLO-010.** Where the regression threshold framework fixed by `PERF-036` and `PERF-037` in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md) overrides a per-dimension baseline through an explicit operator decision recorded in `benches/baselines/overrides.toml` under `PERF-038`, the corresponding SLO baseline under `SLO-001` and `SLO-002` MUST be updated in lock-step. An operator override of the regression baseline that does not propagate to the SLO baseline MUST cause an `slo-baseline-drift` structured event under `THREAT-080` to be emitted, and the drift MUST be resolved before the next compliance-window evaluation.

## 6. Error-budget policy

### 6.1 Normative requirements

- **SLO-011.** For every SLO with a fractional commitment (`SLO-003`, `SLO-004`, `SLO-005`, `SLO-006`, `SLO-007`, `SLO-008`), the error budget over the compliance window MUST be computed as `(1 − SLO_target) × compliance_window`. The compliance window defaults to **30 days** for `SLO-004` (availability) and `SLO-008` (readiness); the compliance window for the rolling-mean SLOs (`SLO-003`, `SLO-005`, `SLO-006`, `SLO-007`) is also **30 days** unless the operator configures a different window in the deployment-specific runbook.

- **SLO-012.** The burn rate against the error budget MUST be computed continuously from the SLI metrics exposed under section 4. A burn rate of `1` over the compliance window indicates that the budget will be exhausted exactly at the end of the window. Burn rates greater than `1` indicate accelerated consumption; burn rates less than `1` indicate slower-than-budget consumption.

- **SLO-013.** Heimdall MUST emit a structured event under `THREAT-080` in [`007-threat-model.md`](007-threat-model.md) when any of the following conditions are first observed within a compliance window:
  - **`slo-burn-fast`**: a burn rate greater than or equal to **14.4** sustained over a **1-hour window** (the "fast burn" alert, calibrated to exhaust the entire 30-day budget within 2 days at the observed rate).
  - **`slo-burn-slow`**: a burn rate greater than or equal to **6** sustained over a **6-hour window** (the "slow burn" alert).
  - **`slo-budget-exhausted`**: the error budget for the current compliance window has been fully consumed.
  - **`slo-budget-restored`**: the rolling compliance window has advanced such that the budget that had previously been classified as exhausted is again above zero.
  Each event MUST identify the SLO under which the burn was observed, the cell, the current burn rate, and the time remaining in the compliance window.

- **SLO-014.** When `slo-budget-exhausted` is emitted under `SLO-013`, the project's "release freeze" gate MUST be engaged for the affected cell. While the freeze is engaged on a cell, no non-security release MUST be tagged that changes the source paths governing the affected cell's hot path. Security releases (per the engineering policies in [`010-engineering-policies.md`](010-engineering-policies.md), in particular `ENG-202` through `ENG-208` on security releases and `THREAT-013` on signed artefacts) are exempt from the freeze gate. The freeze gate MUST be released automatically when an `slo-budget-restored` event is emitted for the same cell and SLO.

- **SLO-015.** A budget exhaustion under `SLO-013` MUST trigger a written post-mortem within **7 calendar days** of the `slo-budget-exhausted` event, irrespective of whether the budget is restored within that window. The post-mortem MUST identify root cause, the cells and SLOs affected, the time to detection, the time to recovery, the corrective actions taken or planned, and the regression-prevention measures applied. The post-mortem MUST be filed as a GitHub Issue tagged `slo:postmortem` and linked to the SLO event identifiers.

- **SLO-016.** Recovery from a budget-exhausted state MUST NOT be obtained by retroactively widening the SLO commitments. A failed SLO is resolved by fixing the underlying defect, by accepting the regression through an explicit operator decision recorded under `PERF-038` and propagated under `SLO-010`, or by removing the affected workload from the cell. SLO targets MUST NOT be edited downward in response to a freeze gate without a coordinated change recorded in both this document and [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md), reviewed under the engineering policies of [`010-engineering-policies.md`](010-engineering-policies.md).

## 7. Reporting cadence

### 7.1 Normative requirements

- **SLO-017.** The project MUST publish, on a rolling **30-day cadence**, an SLO compliance report covering every cell of the matrix and every SLO fixed by section 5. The report MUST be derived from the metrics exposed under `THREAT-081` and `THREAT-083` and from the structured events emitted under `SLO-013`. The report MUST be published at the project's operational dashboard location and SHOULD additionally be summarised in the project's release-notes channel.

- **SLO-018.** The annual revisit fixed by `PERF-026` and `PERF-067` in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md) MUST include a coordinated revisit of the SLO commitments in this document. The revisit MUST: (1) compare observed SLI distributions over the preceding 12 months against the commitments in section 5; (2) identify SLOs that were consistently exceeded (in which case the commitment SHOULD be tightened to maintain forward pressure) and SLOs that were consistently breached (in which case the commitment MUST be either re-affirmed with a remediation plan or revised); (3) record the outcome in a dated changelog entry attached to this document; (4) publish a public retrospective consistent with `PERF-068`.

## 9. Rationale

The SLO framework is the operational projection of the performance-targets framework already fixed by [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md). The performance-targets framework operates at the source-tree side of the system: every change passes through CI regression gates that compare against a calibration baseline, and a regression beyond the thresholds fixed by `PERF-037` fails the build. This is the right surface for catching regressions before they ship, but it is silent on what the deployed service is doing once it is running. SLOs close that loop by expressing commitments at the deployment side: the same dimensions and the same baselines, but evaluated continuously against live traffic, with a structural alerting and freeze policy when the commitment is breached.

Deriving the SLO commitments under `SLO-001` and `SLO-002` directly from the regression thresholds under `PERF-037` keeps the source-tree side and the deployment side coherent by construction. A regression that passes CI (because it is within the threshold) MUST also be within the SLO envelope (because the SLO multiplier equals the CI threshold). A regression that fails CI cannot be deployed in the first place; a regression that passes CI but is observed in production beyond the threshold indicates either an environmental difference between the bench and the deployment or a workload that the calibration corpus did not represent — both of which are operational signals that the SLO is designed to surface. Inventing absolute numerical p99 latency targets (for example, "p99 ≤ 5 ms hot") at the specification level would have been speculation: the performance-targets framework explicitly defers absolute numbers to the Sprint 35 calibration under `PERF-035`, and the SLO framework therefore inherits that deferral. The multiplier-over-baseline form (`1.10 × baseline`, `1.15 × baseline`) is the only form that is honest about what the project has actually decided versus what it has deferred to empirical calibration.

The error-rate SLO at 0.1% under `SLO-003` is set to be tight enough that a sustained regression in the validator, in the upstream-failure handling, or in the parser is visible quickly, while not being so tight that natural variation in upstream availability tips the SLO into chronic violation. Excluding `bogus`-driven `SERVFAIL` from the error-rate numerator (via the `SLI-009` decomposition) is required because a `bogus` outcome is a policy-correct response to a misconfigured or adversarial zone: the client receives `SERVFAIL`, but the server has done exactly what `DNSSEC-013` requires. Counting `bogus`-driven `SERVFAIL` as an error would penalise Heimdall for the public DNS being broken, which is not a service-level failure on Heimdall's part. The sub-counter under `SLI-009` preserves the operator's ability to distinguish the two cases, which is required for diagnosis.

The availability SLO at "four nines" (99.99%) over 30 days under `SLO-004` is calibrated against the deployment posture of mature DNS infrastructure. Authoritative servers in the public DNS, recursive resolvers in large operator networks, and managed-DNS providers all operate at four-to-five-nines availability in production; Heimdall's positioning as a high-performance, security-focused server requires that its commitment be at least at the four-nines level. The 5-minute window granularity matches the convention in mature SLO frameworks (Google SRE Workbook, the `slo-generator` tooling, the OpenSLO specification) and matches the natural cadence of Prometheus scraping; smaller windows would over-weight transient noise and larger windows would under-weight short incidents. Defining the per-window availability predicate as "at least 99% of admitted queries answered within the cell's p99 target" rather than as a pure "queries returned an answer" predicate ties availability to latency: a cell that returns answers but at twice its p99 commitment is not available from the user's perspective, and the SLO framework reflects that.

The cache hit-rate SLOs under `SLO-005` and `SLO-006` are intentionally set at different floors for the recursive resolver (80%) and for the forwarder (70%) because the two roles populate their caches through different resolution paths, as already noted in the rationale of [`004-cache-policy.md`](004-cache-policy.md). The recursive resolver walks the hierarchy and benefits from negative caching, aggressive NSEC/NSEC3 synthesis, and a broader population of names; the forwarder serves a narrower upstream-driven population whose cacheability is dominated by the upstream's TTLs. Setting the forwarder floor lower honours that structural difference and prevents a false SLO violation under a workload that the role serves correctly.

The DNSSEC validation rate SLO under `SLO-007` is split into a ceiling on `bogus` (≤ 0.5%) and a complement floor on the sum of the other three outcomes (≥ 99.5%). The ceiling on `bogus` is the operator-visible signal of a regression: if the rate of `bogus` outcomes climbs, either Heimdall's validator has regressed against a class of well-signed zones or a class of upstream signers has regressed against the public DNS, and either case is an operational signal worth raising. The complement floor captures the same constraint from the other side and makes the SLO symmetric. The 0.5% number is not arbitrary: it is calibrated against the empirical bogus rate observed by public-DNS measurement projects (RIPE Atlas, B-Root, equivalent open-access archives) over the preceding decade, which is consistently below 0.5% on a healthy public-DNS workload.

The readiness SLO under `SLO-008` is intentionally one nine lower than the availability SLO (99.95% versus 99.99%) because the readiness predicate fixed by `OPS-024` and `OPS-042` includes external dependencies — Redis reachability, forwarder upstream reachability, DNSSEC trust-anchor initialisation — whose failure is not always within Heimdall's control. Holding readiness to the same standard as in-process availability would punish Heimdall for failures of components it depends on; relaxing readiness by one nine recognises that distinction. The same SLI is the right signal for orchestrators (Kubernetes, systemd) because they consume the `GET /readyz` endpoint directly; aligning their consumption with the SLO framework keeps the platform-level health signal and the service-level commitment coherent.

The error-budget policy under section 6 implements the standard SRE multi-window multi-burn-rate alerting pattern (Google SRE Workbook, chapter 5). The two alert windows fixed by `SLO-013` — 1-hour fast burn at rate 14.4 and 6-hour slow burn at rate 6 — are calibrated to give the operator early-warning signal of fast incidents without false-positive alerts on transient noise, and to give a slower signal on chronic degradation that would not trip the fast-burn threshold but would still exhaust the budget. The release-freeze gate under `SLO-014` is the structural enforcement that an exhausted budget is treated as a halt-the-line condition: a cell that is consistently failing its SLO must not absorb additional source-tree change until the underlying cause is resolved. The exemption for security releases is required because security risk is not reducible to SLO compliance: a cell with an exhausted budget that is also subject to an active CVE remains subject to the security release obligations of [`010-engineering-policies.md`](010-engineering-policies.md).

The reporting cadence under section 7 ties the SLO framework to the project governance. The 30-day rolling cadence under `SLO-017` matches the compliance-window definition and gives operators a continuously updated view rather than a stale calendar-month report. The annual revisit under `SLO-018` is coupled to the comparison-cycle revisit fixed by `PERF-026` and `PERF-067` so that the SLO framework, the performance-targets framework, and the comparative-benchmark framework all move in lock-step. Decoupling the revisits would create a drift surface; coupling them keeps the three frameworks coherent across cycles.

Strict no-aggregation under `SLO-009` is the same structural-gating choice already adopted for role activation in [`001-server-roles.md`](001-server-roles.md), for transport-listener instantiation in [`002-transports.md`](002-transports.md), and for regression detection per dimension under `PERF-036` in [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md). Aggregation across cells or across SLOs would hide failures behind successes elsewhere and would defeat the purpose of having a per-cell, per-dimension framework in the first place.

## 10. Open questions

The framework fixed by sections 4 through 7 is complete given the calibration baseline established by [`008-performance-targets.md`](008-performance-targets.md). The items below remain open and MUST NOT be assumed before they are decided.

- **Absolute per-cell p99 and p99.9 baselines.** Under `SLO-001` and `SLO-002`, the SLO commitments are expressed as multipliers over the cell's calibrated baseline. The absolute numerical baselines per cell are produced by the Sprint 35 calibration run under `PERF-033` and `PERF-034` and stored in `benches/baselines/`. They are not duplicated in this document and they are not directly authored here; their authority lies in the calibration artefact. This open question is resolved when the calibration artefact is committed and the operational dashboard begins consuming it.
- **Workload-representative calibration corpus.** Whether the calibration corpus fixed by `PERF-043` is representative of the deployment workload at any given operator site is a question that the operator must answer; deployments whose workload departs materially from the calibration corpus may experience SLI distributions that legitimately differ from the calibrated baseline. The mechanism by which an operator records a deployment-specific override of the SLO baseline, analogous to the source-tree override under `PERF-038`, is not yet specified.
- **SLO target for the authoritative-role error-rate SLI.** `SLO-003` fixes a 0.1% error-rate ceiling uniformly across cells. Whether the authoritative-server role should carry a stricter ceiling (because authoritative responses are deterministic functions of the served zone data, with no upstream-failure axis) is open.
- **Cache hit-rate SLO during attack windows.** `SLO-005` and `SLO-006` acknowledge that random-subdomain attacks and NXDOMAIN floods will transiently drop hit rates below the floor. The precise rule for excluding "under-attack" windows from the rolling-mean calculation is not yet specified; current text relies on the rolling 30-day mean to absorb transient excursions.
- **Composition with multi-instance deployments.** This document defines SLIs and SLOs per single Heimdall instance. The composition rule for an operator running a fleet of instances (sum, mean, weighted by traffic, worst-case-per-cell?) is not yet specified.
- **Public-SLO publication.** Whether the project itself publishes an externally-visible SLO compliance report (analogous to a public DNS provider's status page) versus restricting `SLO-017` reports to internal consumption is not yet decided. The reporting cadence is fixed; the audience is not.
